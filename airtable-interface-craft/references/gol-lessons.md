# What made the EA refinement work

This case study captures specific successes the first six-bullet recap omitted. Apply the mechanisms where they fit; keep the client's business rules configurable.

## Visual craft and useful delight

1. Preserved the loved house/car neighborhood instead of treating refinement as a redesign.
2. Used consistent warm colors, rounded shapes, typography, borders and shadows across the scene and controls.
3. Crafted small details: arched doors, flower boxes, soft clouds, porch light, a selection star and the heart on the larger car.
4. Separated green ground from blue sky; reserved space for smaller houses and softened labels so text did not cover the road/car.
5. Planned layers and non-interactive decoration so art did not steal clicks or obstruct the moving car.
6. Tied car movement to the selected application and celebrations to saved completion, with cleanup and reduced-motion behavior.
7. Preserved the status color when selecting a house and added a separate selection indicator. The legend and text made color understandable.

## Practical navigation and readability

8. Kept a searchable application list alongside clickable houses, both selecting the same family.
9. Increased important writing and action sizes, checked contrast/dark mode, and made all five mobile tabs reachable.
10. Used progressive disclosure: family selection, application tabs, expandable subtasks and compact document details.
11. Added meaningful signing-date sorting/ranges and task/phase/status filters; filtering did not reduce the running point total.
12. Showed all applicants' full names and their own training evidence, with primary applicant above co-applicant. Search included both people.
13. Joined saved training, task dates, holidays and labelled last-day planning notes in the calendar without treating a scheduled class as completed training.

## Accurate data and reliable actions

14. Read real source/schema/SDK declarations and used verified IDs and field types.
15. Validated parent/child/application/template relationships and deduplicated stable record IDs rather than matching approximate names.
16. Kept another staff member's parent as context for an owned subtask without granting a completion action. Preserved approved role exceptions.
17. Treated missing data, contradictory completion metadata, save failure and uncertain acknowledgement as distinct states.
18. Made unchecking reverse the full completion state and its points; repeated checking did not multiply earnings.
19. Delayed confetti, points and File attached feedback until the corresponding saved evidence was available.
20. Used native authenticated attachment capabilities, with explicit applicant choice and exact document/application/applicant links.
21. Preserved files/notes, reused matching tracker records, handled existing duplicates and gave taskless applications a Documents tab.
22. Simplified the staff action without removing the linked storage behind it: choose a document, download the template and upload the completed file.

## Collaboration, troubleshooting and delivery

23. Split scene, data, documents and tests into owned pieces with typed contracts, then assembled one complete paste-ready source centrally.
24. Checked failures and recovery, mobile layouts and realistic long names with fictional records. Reviewed harness assumptions instead of treating every mock failure as a product bug.
25. Distinguished source problems from missing Data selections, native layout omissions, permissions and broken local dependency paths.
26. Diagnosed the stalled in-app source editor and used the supported Chrome editor that worked, without weakening security.
27. Duplicated the interface and a shared native document layout; fixed missing editable Attachments and the broken display only in the copy.
28. Preserved prior sources and GitHub history, independently copied the saved source back out of Airtable, and confirmed exact equality before publication.
29. Published only after the user requested it, then checked the published collaborator view.
30. Kept real family screenshots outside Git and distinguished local tests, installation, publication and live record actions in the handoff.

## Project-specific choices

EA used green for no open assigned tasks, yellow for open tasks, red for overdue work and purple for explicitly closed cases. Closure took priority and was based on actual exit statuses, not a guessed meaning of phase/Hold/Active.

The approved running total awarded 5 points per completed task and 1 per subtask, derived from saved eligible rows before filtering. This was not a permanent lifetime ledger. Reopening was manual. Linked quality performance indicators were visible labels; obligation propagation was unnecessary. Signing/referral/visit/class/approval anchors and holiday rules were project decisions, not plugin defaults.

The full source was built and locally exercised against the actual Interface SDK. In the October 9 installation, complete saved-source readback matched the delivery, the separate page was published, and its live document controls rendered. Actual binary upload and live task completion were not tested in that installation/publication check. Targeted accessibility checks do not establish full certification.

This package contains no live base/field/record identifiers, private family screenshots, attachment URLs or credentials. The case study explains the reusable approach rather than shipping the client's source.
