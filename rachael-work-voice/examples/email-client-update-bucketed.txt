Locked 2026-07-18 by Rachael as the canonical shape for a "here's what landed" email to a monthly-retainer client (CC, THFH, GoL). Bucketed by project, concrete counts, decision points with verbatim examples, client team to-do.

Structure this exemplifies:

1. Salutation — "Hi <Name>," (never "Hi team")
2. Project frame — one line naming which project buckets the updates fall under (anchors the client to the roadmap, not just tasks)
3. Per-bucket sections — one section per work bucket. Section header on its own line. Then:
   - What landed (concrete counts — ~1,000 records, 155 regions, 35 certs, not "several" or "many")
   - Attached csv/pdf referenced by filename ("See attached csv, proposal-updated-records.")
   - Follow-on state ("There are now no WIOA regions that are marked as approved but not linked to candidates in those regions.")
   - New helper fields as a → bullet list, one per line
   - "More to follow" line if the work isn't complete
4. Client team to-do section — heading "<Client> Team to-do", then one bullet per action, "Please" prefix, actions the client team owns not you
5. Decision points — when a to-do requires a client choice, frame as "Please decide if you want the instructions to be 1. <option A> or 2. <option B>." and give your recommendation on its own line
6. Verbatim example when the ask is a choice — paste the actual current-state artifact so the client can see what they're deciding about. Makes the ask concrete rather than abstract.
7. Sign-off — "All best," then "Rachael" on the next line

Voice rules that apply on top of the eight moves:
- No throat-clearing before the first fact. Lead with "Below are updates for…".
- Concrete counts everywhere (~1,000, 155, 35).
- Never use metaphor/analogy words (locked 2026-07-17 in CLAUDE.md): no "clean", "heal", "drift", "smoke test/check", "main" for anything but git. Say the literal action.
- Plain English throughout — "would the client understand this without opening Airtable?" is the test.
- No record IDs, no fldXXX, no jargon.
- Skip bold headers.
- Dots (.) for unordered lists, → for annotated bullets.
- End with an invitation or ask, not a summary of what you just said.
