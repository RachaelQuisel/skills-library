# Voice example — internal team ask (Slack DM to XRAY teammates)

Rachael's voice when asking XRAY teammates for input. Locked on August 5, 2026. Rachael edited a prose-heavy draft into this message. She sent it in this form. Bracketed text replaces private names and plans.

This is strong evidence of a writing choice she approved for that message. It does not prove that she still prefers every choice. Do not copy private names, facts, or plans.

## The four things this example is teaching

1. **Concise without being redundant.** Each sentence carries one idea. If a benefit is named once, don't name it again below.
2. **Not pedantic.** No trailing clauses explaining what the reader already understands. No spelling out the implication of a request.
3. **Ask real questions, don't announce preferences.** "Hosting every other week?" not "I was thinking bi-weekly." Every question stays open; the teammate gets to answer, not rubber-stamp.
4. **Short out of respect for their time.** One screen of Slack. If it doesn't fit without scrolling, it's too long.

## The message

```
Hi, [teammates], [teammate] and I were kicking around [initiative] (open office hours where prospects can drop in and ask technical questions), and, since y'all are talking to leads, would you be up for sharing your thoughts, opinions, insights?


Do you think this would be helpful?
What day and time would work for prospects?
Hosting every other week?
What are prospects asking about most


Some benefits:
Feeding [teammate] material for the newsletter and, since I stalk all the frontier AI companies, wowing prospects with the most up-to-date info :joy: Most weeks I'll likely be hanging out by myself, and that's fine; that we offer open hours is another signal of credibility, even when no one shows.



Topic ideas:
The prescient question I'm hearing right now is: if anyone can build, what do consultants still offer?
1. Schema and data design (I haven't had a client who could pull this off on their own); 2. Judgment on scope, i.e. what goes into the MVP vs. saved for later (so much of my job is pruning a client's ideas until what's left is streamlined and focused);
3. Identifying and developing the user journey (I've mapped these enough that I've got an intuitive sense for spotting quick wins and weak points)
```

## Voice moves worth copying

- Colloquial warmth ("y'all", "kicking around")
- Three-word combos over one clinical word ("thoughts, opinions, insights")
- Section labels do structural work ("Some benefits:", "Topic ideas:") — no headers, no bold
- Expressive phrases stay in ("since I stalk all the frontier AI companies") — that's the voice, not filler
- Semicolons inside bullets connect related thoughts
- Positive framing paired with negative ("quick wins and weak points")
- Active verbs ("identifying and developing"), not passive ("reading")
- The source contains an emoji. Do not add emojis unless the user asks for them.
- No sign-off on Slack

## Voice moves to strip when drafting

- "I want your read" / "I'd appreciate your read before I move on it" (mechanical formality)
- "Where I need you:" (client-email framing, wrong voice for teammates)
- Prose paragraphs that hide questions inside sentences
- Any sentence that explains a sentence already written
- Any "I was thinking X" that turns a real question into a stated preference
- "All best, Rachael" on chat-style comms
