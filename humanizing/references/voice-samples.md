# Writing Samples From Rachael's Voice Guide

Copied from `~/Claude-Work/ABOUT-ME/my-voice.md` on May 24, 2026. Bracketed text replaces private names, figures, or project details. The remaining wording and punctuation are preserved.

Rachael selected these samples for her voice guide. That makes them useful evidence of the writing she wanted the guide to represent. It does not prove that she wrote every word without assistance. It does not prove that she still prefers every choice.

Use recurring patterns. Do not copy private facts from a sample.

## Sample 1: Client update (Slack, to leadership)

> Quick updates re: [client]
>
> I'll have the revised project timeline completed and in Gantt view by Monday morning, so you'll have a visual when pitching a longer contract. The vibe I got was definitely one of commitment and excitement.
>
> Got a (relatively) measurable pain point: [client contact] currently spends several hours/week copy-pasting data from IOs into QB for batch invoicing. She's also hand-typing notes that bundle multiple data types together, which means that info is stuck in free text and can't be used for reporting or analysis.
>
> As always, if I'm off course, please steer me in the right direction. Thanks again for your support and don't hesitate to lmk what I can be doing better to make this a smoother process for all.

## Sample 2: Internal win + next steps (Slack)

> Dropping a little win here: I followed up with an hourly client re: hours recovered after implementing the tool I built, and he responded [time recovered].
>
> On the low-end, that's [annual value] my client can put towards growth and other stuff besides office management labor.
>
> An artifact that captures data-driven wins like this is one thing I'll work on so all hourly engineers can easily use it. If you already know of a follow-up survey or Qs to ask in exit interview with hourly client, please lmk so I can update our inventory.

## Sample 3: Case study blurb (written for sales team)

> We worked with a health and wellness coach who had course content but no program structure. No enrollment model, no cohorts, no way to track participants. Over four months we served as her fractional CTO: designed a two-track program model, built a waitlist system, automated everything from participant agreement through course enrollment to ongoing follow-up emails, and gave her a self-service way to launch new cohorts from Airtable that appear on her website automatically. Three cohorts launched on schedule and under budget. The whole system runs without manual intervention.

> Key win: Showed [client contact] how Airtable lets us keep data in separate fields while also auto-generating a combined field. They get what they want, a notes field text blob in the [client run sheet], and we get complete data for formulas.

## Sample 4: Vibe check to manager (DM)

> Left on a very high note; they were delighted by the run sheet prototype! Thanks again for suggesting I build just a prototype to share (rather than jumping straight into airtable). They are pushing to finish the complete build within the 30 days though I 100% agree re: unrealistic (even if it were a normal build where there's no intermediary step before sunsetting the main CRM platform).

## Sample 5: Sharing an idea (DM, longer form)

> If memory serves, you invited the team to share thoughts re: [internal product]? I have three ideas (apologies for the wall of text and, of course, no rush responding)
>
> My idea: sharing quick wins that can't easily be found via Youtube/blogs/LLMs. I've noticed multiple gaps in knowledge that even expert-level ppl don't know. Things that, when I ask any LLM, they don't know the best answer/get it completely wrong.
