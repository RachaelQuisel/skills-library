# Design rationale and sources

These notes inform design and revision. They do not establish that this plugin has demonstrated clinical or productivity benefits.

## Cognitive accessibility

The W3C Working Group Note [Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/) covers clear content, limiting interruptions, helping users recover context, reducing memory demands, personalization, and testing with users.

Our application to conversational assistance: make the next action visible, keep deferred work recoverable, provide restart notes, and allow different levels of detail. The W3C document primarily addresses web content; adapting its patterns to chat is a design decision rather than a claim of certification.

## ADHD context

[NIMH's ADHD in Adults: 4 Things to Know](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/adhd-what-you-need-to-know) describes difficulties that can involve organization, time management, remembering tasks, and finishing projects. Use user-stated needs rather than assuming every reader has the same difficulties or preferences.

## Exploratory task-management research

Chen, Meng, and Nie's ["Not Just Me and My To-Do List"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258), version 2, describes interviews with 22 ADHD-identifying adults and evaluation of speculative AI concepts with 20 additional participants. It informs attention to user control, emotional burden, and support around interruptions. It is not a trial of this plugin.

Prefer small observed improvements from realistic use over adding more rules or importing entire manuals. Record which behavior changed and why when revising the skill.
