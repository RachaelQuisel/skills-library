# Core Concepts: Business Requirements

Business requirements define the context and goals that drive user and functional requirements.

## The Four Key Components
1.  **Business Opportunity**: The problem or market need being addressed (e.g., "manual tracking leads to expired stock").
2.  **Business Objectives**: Quantitative and measurable goals (e.g., "reduce purchasing costs by 25% within one year").
3.  **Success Metrics**: KPIs to measure if a project is on track *during or shortly after launch* (e.g., "track 60% of containers within 4 weeks").
4.  **Vision Statement**: A concise summary of the long-term intent of the product.

## Product Vision vs. Project Scope
*   **Product Vision**: The ultimate, fully-realized version of the product. It changes slowly as the business strategy evolves.
*   **Project Scope**: The specific portion of the vision that a particular project or iteration will address. It identifies what is **in** and what is **out** for the current release.

## Business Risks vs. Project Risks
*   **Business Risks**: Risks associated with developing (or *not* developing) the product, such as market competition, user acceptance, or timing issues.
*   **Project Risks**: Execution-related risks like budget, schedule, staffing, or technology factors.

## Project Priorities: The Five Dimensions
When making trade-offs, every project has five dimensions that must be categorized:
1.  **Constraint**: A limiting factor the PM must work within (e.g., "must ship by Dec 31").
2.  **Driver**: A significant success objective (e.g., "quality/data integrity is critical").
3.  **Degree of Freedom**: A factor that can be adjusted to balance the others (e.g., "we can defer features to meet the date").

*Note: You cannot treat all five dimensions as constraints!*
